Less, but better

Simplifying is not about subtraction. It is about deciding what truly matters.

Reducing things often feels like losing something: fewer choices, fewer tools, fewer goals. But when you look closely, the real work of simplifying is not subtraction. It is selection.

Every new option leaves a small open tab in our minds. Unfinished tasks, articles we plan to read later, and vague goals all compete for space. Eventually, we spend more time arranging the desk than making anything on it.

A useful filtering question for me has been this: does this add energy to my life today, or does it merely take up space? The answer is not always easy. Still, the question makes the weight I carry out of habit visible.

Less, but better, is not a promise of perfect order. It is a practice of returning attention to what genuinely matters. Sometimes that means deleting an app, saying no to a project, or simply waiting without adding anything new.
